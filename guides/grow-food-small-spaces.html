Home & Garden guide

How to grow food at home, even in a small space

You do not need a farm to grow some of your own food. A sunny window, a balcony or a corner of the yard is enough to start with herbs and leafy greens, and to learn what works in your space before spending more.

Start with light

Light matters more than space. Most fruiting plants, such as tomatoes and peppers, need around 6 to 8 hours of direct sun a day. Leafy greens and many herbs cope with less. If your windows get little sun, a simple LED grow light can make indoor growing possible.

Pick easy crops first

Lettuce, spinach, basil, mint, green onions and microgreens grow fast and forgive mistakes. Starting with them gives you quick results and helps you learn watering and light needs before trying harder plants.

Containers and soil

Use containers with drainage holes and a good potting mix, not garden soil, which compacts in pots. Bigger containers dry out more slowly, so they need less frequent watering.
