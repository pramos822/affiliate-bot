Blackout preparedness: a simple checklist for your home

Power outages can last hours or days. A little preparation makes them much easier to handle, and most of it costs less than people expect.

Water and food

The common guideline from emergency agencies such as Ready.gov is to store at least one gallon of water per person per day, for at least three days, plus a three-day supply of food that does not need cooking or refrigeration.

Light, power and information

Keep flashlights or headlamps with spare batteries, a battery or hand-crank radio, and power banks charged for your phone. Avoid candles where possible because of the fire risk.

Health and safety

Have a first aid kit, a supply of any prescription medicines, and copies of important documents. Never run a generator or grill indoors or in a garage: carbon monoxide is a real danger during outages.

Going further with DIY plans and kits

For people who want to go beyond the basics, these guides and kits cover blackout protection, emergency water and simple home alarms. Electrical and water projects can be risky: get professional help when in doubt.
